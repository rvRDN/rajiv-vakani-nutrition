## Decision problem

> I have obesity (or I am searching for weight-loss options). What actually helps, and where does each option sit among everything else that has been studied?

Readers arrive after searching the condition. Many already live with years of diets, regain, stigma, a medication they cannot get or cannot stay on, supplement and “fat-burner” claims, surgery they were told to consider or to fear, and a newer incretin conversation that sounds like either a miracle or a scam. The desperate-reader test applies: someone who has tried lifestyle changes, switched drugs, or chased natural products deserves the full honest landscape of what was studied, what each option shows, and where it fits, not a top-three shortlist and not a dismissal of “unofficial” therapies before the evidence speaks.

## Starting population / assumptions

- Begin with the **broad clinical population appropriate to obesity** (typically adults with overweight or obesity as the main literature spine). Include age-specific, pediatric/adolescent, pregnancy-related, or class-of-obesity evidence when it is common, decision-relevant, or map-changing; do not lock an adult-only or pediatric rail in Step 0.  
- **Standard of care is on the map**, not a silent backdrop. Lifestyle foundations, common anti-obesity medicine classes (including incretin therapies that surface), behavioral programs, and metabolic/bariatric surgery earn investigation as decision objects or clear comparator seats. This campaign is not a “non-drug only” map and not a “GLP-1 only” map. BMI cutoffs, comorbidity-triggered eligibility, and surgery thresholds are teaching context and claim hygiene, not a Step 0 architecture lock.  
- Diet patterns, calorie and formula strategies, exercise, sleep, supplements, herbs, devices, procedures that surface, and home remedies sit beside that spine and are calibrated by strongest earned claim. Popular weak claims are investigated before any public “not first” section.  
- **Plant-based analogue check applies when appropriate:** keep strong studies for animal-derived options on the map, and when an animal-derived food, supplement, or therapy is on the map, also look for the plant-based version readers will ask about. Report what exists (or that it does not). Do not invent transfer from biochemical similarity. Canonical rule: [`.cursor/rules/plant-based-analogue-check.mdc`](../../.cursor/rules/plant-based-analogue-check.mdc).  
- The product still educates and places options. It does not write a treatment algorithm or recommend a winner.

## Research discipline (not architecture)

Search and claim-hygiene rules for Phases 0–3. They do **not** add exclusions, object families, or reader architecture.

1. **Broad clinical obesity first, phenotype-aware throughout.** Architecture waits until evidence shows whether the landscape can remain unified. Do not turn this into a lifestyle-only, surgery-only, or incretin-only map in Phase 0.  
2. **Weight change is not a footnote, and it is not a hard outcome.** Kilograms, percent weight loss, waist, and composition can change who was enrolled and what “worked.” Sources and later objects need enough measurement context that synthesis can distinguish weight change from diabetes remission, sleep-apnea change, cardiovascular events, or death.  
3. **Surrogate endpoints need honest grain.** Weight loss is not the same as heart attack, stroke, or death reduction. SELECT-class findings do not automatically transfer to every anti-obesity medicine or to every lifestyle program. Durability and regain are part of the claim, not a later editorial add-on.  
4. **Guideline eras and eligibility are claim hygiene.** A finding from a pre-incretin era does not automatically transfer to a GLP-1-era map. Prefer current major guidelines and systematic reviews first (through the campaign start date), and use older landmark evidence only where it still carries the claim.  
5. **Do not import another campaign’s architecture.** Type 2 diabetes’s remission / DiRECT / SURPASS-CVOT shape is that map’s finding. This campaign collects obesity-without-diabetes evidence on its own terms. Emerging Therapies dossiers are companions, not seats, until this library earns them.  
6. **Plant-based when appropriate, not as a parallel ideology.** Include strong studies as usual. When an animal-derived intervention is on the map, check the analogue readers will ask about. Absence is a useful finding. Do not upgrade a thin analogue trial into the main claim.  
7. **The likely-object table stays provisional.** Do not add families in Step 0 because they will probably surface. Phase 0 discovers them.
