# Metabolic surgery and endoscopic procedures · Thinking Artifact v0.1

**Status:** Frozen v1.0 · August 13, 2026  
**Type:** Thinking artifact (orientation)  
**Evidence Stability Check:** [`./evidence-stability-check-v0.1.md`](./evidence-stability-check-v0.1.md) (**Frozen v1.0**)  
**Object:** Metabolic/bariatric surgery and endoscopic bariatric procedures as one object with two mandatory named branches  
**Intellectual shape:** **On-map procedures; surgery and endoscopy as non-collapsed branches; short-term incretin annotates; SOS observational, not SELECT-class.**

**Accepted evidence used:** C3 De Luca 2026 *Obesity* GRADE NMA; A3 NICE NG246. A1 surgery-out-of-scope is a coverage note, not a veto. **Working companion (not seated):** Sjöström et al. SOS 2007 *NEJM* and later life-expectancy follow-up.  
**Stance:** Surgery earns a long-term weight seat among studied procedures. Endoscopy is a distinct and generally weaker branch (except ESG). Short-term incretin comparison annotates. SOS does not become SELECT.  
**Job:** Hold four difficult pieces at once: long-term surgical superiority, endoscopic non-collapse, short-term medicine annotation without merger, and observational mortality without CVOT clothes.

**Evidentiary shape (do not flatten in later phases):** GRADE network of surgery, endoscopy, and medicines with a mandatory two-branch split and an observational companion. Not a shots-replaced-surgery story. Not a SELECT-class randomized warrant. Not a seventh endoscopic object.

**Working question:** How do you keep a procedures seat when short-term incretin weight can look like some surgery, endoscopy is not surgery, long-term endoscopic data are missing, and the best-known mortality signal is observational?

## Orientation: four pieces, not one pile

**C3 is the seated network.** Search to 1 December 2024; 139 RCTs (54 MBS, n=61,961; 21 EBP, n=2,934; 64 OMM, n=5,991). Most surgeries and tirzepatide produce TBWL >10% at 26–52 weeks. Metabolic/bariatric surgery appears superior for long-term weight, especially RYGB, sleeve, SADI, and BPD. Endoscopic procedures except ESG were less effective than newer obesity medicines. Semaglutide and tirzepatide showed no inferior short-term weight results versus some surgery. Long-term data are lacking for most medicines and for all endoscopic procedures. SAE costs are higher with EBP/MBS than with medicines; BPD has the highest long-term SAE.

That last sentence travels with the long-term weight lead. Highest-effect surgery is not therefore first-line.

**Two branches, not one.** Surgery ≠ endoscopy. C3 already distinguishes them (effectiveness versus newer medicines; SAE profile; absence of long-term EBP data). The object-index lock forbids collapse into “surgery-lite.” It also forbids promoting endoscopy to a seventh object from this artifact. Procedure types stay internal to the surgery branch (RYGB, sleeve, SADI, BPD). ESG, balloons, and neighbors stay internal to the endoscopic branch. ESG’s exception is a named note, not a reason to feature endoscopy as if it inherited surgery’s long-term grain.

**Short-term incretin comparison annotates.** Semaglutide and tirzepatide looking non-inferior to some surgery at 26–52 weeks is a real C3 node. It does not merge this object with obesity medicines. It does not retire long-term MBS. Long-term data lacking for most OMM is the other half of that node.

**SOS is a working companion, not D1.** Sjöström 2007: 4,047 people; non-randomized; 10.9 years; adjusted mortality HR 0.71. Later life-expectancy follow-up HR 0.77 and about three years longer median life expectancy versus control. Important. Observational. Mixed diabetes status historically. It does not become a SELECT-class randomized CVOT by being the best-known surgical mortality signal. Do not seat it from this artifact.

A1’s surgery-out-of-scope line is that algorithm’s coverage boundary. A3 and C3 put procedures on this map.

## Boundaries to carry forward

**Applies most clearly to:** Adults weighing surgery or an endoscopic procedure against each other and against medicines.

**Does not earn:** SELECT-class randomized MACE for surgery from SOS; collapse of endoscopy; merge with obesity medicines; a seventh endoscopic object; seating SOS.

**Keep uncertain:** Long-term RCTs of endoscopic procedures; long-term RCTs of newer medicines versus surgery; how loudly SOS mortality may speak once claim-type honesty is locked; how loudly ESG should be featured among endoscopic options.

---

## Bottom line for later synthesis

1. Keep C3’s procedures seat: long-term surgical weight superiority among studied operations.  
2. Keep surgery and endoscopy as non-collapsed named branches. ESG is an exception, not an inheritance of long-term surgery.  
3. Keep short-term incretin comparison as annotation. Do not merge with obesity medicines. Do not retire surgery.  
4. Keep SAE costs, including BPD’s long-term SAE, inside the claim.  
5. Keep SOS observational. Do not dress it as SELECT.  
6. Library stays at 8. A1 out-of-scope is a coverage note, not a veto.

**Strongest earned claim (carried verbatim from the accepted Stability Check §12):**

> Metabolic surgery and endoscopic procedures earn an on-map seat from a GRADE network of medicines, endoscopic procedures, and surgery (C3): surgery appears superior for long-term weight (especially RYGB, sleeve, SADI, BPD), endoscopic procedures except ESG were less effective than newer medicines, semaglutide and tirzepatide showed no inferior short-term weight results versus some surgery, SAE costs are higher with procedures than medicines and highest long-term with BPD, SOS observational mortality is a mandatory working companion rather than a SELECT-class randomized CVOT, endoscopic and surgical branches must not collapse, short-term incretin comparison annotates rather than merging this object with obesity medicines, and the library stays at 8.
