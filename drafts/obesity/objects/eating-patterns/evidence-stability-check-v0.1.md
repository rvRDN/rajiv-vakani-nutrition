# Eating patterns for obesity · Evidence Stability Check v0.1

**Status:** Frozen v1.0 · August 13, 2026  
**Type:** Evidence Stability Check (internal peer review)  
**Artifact:** [`./thinking-artifact-v0.1.md`](./thinking-artifact-v0.1.md) (**Frozen v1.0**)  
**Object:** Named dietary programmes and macronutrient patterns for adults with overweight or obesity, with VLCD / formula diets as a mandatory named internal branch  
**First conclusion under review:** Eating patterns earn a pattern-level weight and risk-factor seat (C1) in which low-carbohydrate and low-fat are similar at six months, named-diet differences are small, and 12-month fade is part of the claim, while VLCD/formula diets stay an internal branch calibrated with working evidence rather than DiRECT or a seventh object.

**Accepted evidence used:** C1 Ge 2020 *BMJ* NMA; A1 AACE 2025; A2 Obesity Canada 2025 (lifestyle-conjunction placement); A3 NICE NG246. **Working evidence (not seated) for the VLCD branch:** Parretti et al., 2016 *Obes Rev* (VLED plus behavioural programme vs behavioural programme alone). Do not import DiRECT from the type 2 diabetes campaign.

**Combine risk:** “Eat less” as one interchangeable warrant, or treating a six-month named-diet rank as a 12-month winner or as proven MI/stroke prevention.

**Inflation risk:** SUCRA-style ranks as event ranks; Parretti’s extra kilograms as a DiRECT-style remission story.  
**Unit the evidence suggests:** One **pattern-level weight/risk-factor** object with VLCD internal and weight ≠ events.

## 2. Confidence

| Claim | Confidence | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Low-carbohydrate and low-fat produce similar six-month weight loss vs usual diet | Moderate (C1 moderate certainty on those comparisons) | C1: 4.63 vs 4.37 kg at six months; 121 trials; 21,942 people |
| Named-diet differences at six months are small | Moderate | C1: Atkins 5.5 kg, Zone 4.1 kg, DASH 3.6 kg vs usual diet; between-diet gaps smaller than diet vs no diet |
| Weight loss diminished at 12 months across patterns | High as C1’s durability grain | C1: fade at 12 months; risk-factor benefits largely disappeared except Mediterranean |
| Pattern ranking is hard-outcome ranking | Low, reject | C1 is weight and risk factors, not events |
| Vegetarian/Ornish-class programmes are on the map | Moderate as presence, not as transferred Mediterranean | C1 included them among 14 named programmes |
| Unstudied vegan-only plates inherit Mediterranean’s remaining 12-month risk-factor signal | Low, reject | Plant-based honesty rule |
| VLCD plus a behavioural programme adds weight loss vs a behavioural programme alone at one year | Moderate as labeled working evidence | Parretti 2016: −3.9 kg (−6.7 to −1.1) at 12 months; −1.4 kg at 24 months; −1.3 kg at 38–60 months (CI includes little/no difference at the longest follow-up) |
| VLCD is DiRECT | Low, reject | Different campaign, eligibility, and remission job |
| VLCD must become a seventh object | Low, reject for now | Branch can be calibrated; split gate closed |
| A3 places dietary advice and, in specialist settings, low-energy formula strategies | High for placement | Pathway ≠ C1 event upgrade |

**Disagreement lives in:** How loudly to name Atkins’s six-month lead given small between-diet gaps; how to teach 12-month fade without saying “diets do not work”; and how to keep VLCD visible without importing type 2 diabetes remission architecture.

## 3. Alternative interpretations

**A. "Atkins won six months, so it is the obesity diet."**  
Too flat. C1’s between-diet differences are small; 12-month fade applies across patterns.

**B. "All diets fail at 12 months, so delete the object."**  
Fade is the grain, not a reason to unsit a crowded decision. People still choose among patterns.

**C. "Mediterranean’s remaining risk-factor signal at 12 months is a CVOT."**  
Risk-factor remainder ≠ MI/stroke/death.

**D. "Promote VLCD to its own object, or import DiRECT."**  
Object index locks a branch first. DiRECT is the other campaign’s protocol landmark.

**E. "Merge with behavioural programmes because Ge adjusted for behavioural support."**  
That adjustment is why they are different decisions: diet quality ≠ programme delivery.

## 5. Magnitude

C1 (Ge 2020): 121 trials; 21,942 people; 14 named programmes. Vs usual diet at six months, low-carbohydrate 4.63 kg and low-fat 4.37 kg (both moderate certainty). Named-diet examples: Atkins 5.5 kg, Zone 4.1 kg, DASH 3.6 kg. Weight loss diminished at 12 months. Risk-factor benefits largely disappeared except Mediterranean.

Parretti 2016 (working, VLCD branch): VLED plus behavioural programme vs behavioural programme alone, −3.9 kg at one year, −1.4 kg at 24 months. Premature discontinuation similar (19% vs 20%). Not a seated C. Not DiRECT.

A1/A3 place dietary patterns and, for A3, specialist low-energy strategies with long-term support. Placement ≠ event upgrade.

That magnitude supports **a comparative pattern map with durability humility plus a formula-diet branch**, not a named-diet championship and not a hard-outcome diet.

## 7. Boundary conditions

**Applies most clearly to:** Adults with overweight or obesity choosing among named dietary patterns or considering a medically supervised formula/VLCD protocol.

**Does not earn:** Hard-outcome prevention from named diets; vegan-only transfer; DiRECT import; VLCD as a seventh object from this check; merger with behavioural programmes or exercise.

**Uncertainty:** Trials after C1’s 2018 search cutoff; commercial formula products vs trial VLEDs; eating-disorder risk as safety context.

## 9. Final stress test

1. **Why keep patterns if 12-month differences shrink?**  
   Because the six-month choice is still a real decision, and fade is what the evidence earned.

2. **Does Atkins’s 5.5 kg force a winner?**  
   No. Between-diet gaps are small.

3. **Does Parretti’s −3.9 kg force a VLCD object?**  
   No. It calibrates the branch. Longer follow-up shrinks the difference.

4. **Should vegetarian become a plant-based ideology object?**  
   No. It is a named-programme presence inside C1.

## 11. Missing evidence

1. Named-diet networks with longer than 12-month GRADE comparable to C1.  
2. VLCD vs modern incretin background, not only vs older behavioural programmes.  
3. Hard-outcome trials of named patterns in obesity without diabetes.

**Unresolved before object freeze:** Phase 2 must keep 12-month fade from becoming either “any diet is fine forever” or “diets do not work.”

---

## 12. Strongest earned claim

**Eating patterns earn a pattern-level weight and risk-factor seat (C1): low-carbohydrate and low-fat are similar at six months, named-diet differences are small, weight loss diminished at 12 months, vegetarian/Ornish-class programmes are on the map rather than invented from Mediterranean evidence, that ranking is not hard-outcome ranking, VLCD/formula diets stay a mandatory internal branch calibrated with working evidence (about 4 kg extra at one year vs a behavioural programme alone, smaller later) without becoming DiRECT or a seventh object, and the library stays at 8.**
