## What this product is

An **evidence landscape and decision aid**, not an essay, guideline, or recommendation engine.

Readers come here after searching obesity, weight loss, BMI, semaglutide, tirzepatide, Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, gastric sleeve, gastric bypass, endoscopy, Atkins, keto, Mediterranean diet, formula diet, VLCD, WW, NHS weight-management, green tea, Garcinia, or a related phrase. Many already live with years of diets, regain, stigma, a medicine they cannot get or cannot stay on, supplement and “fat-burner” claims, surgery they were told to consider or to fear, and a newer incretin conversation that sounds like either a miracle or a scam. Some are preparing for a clinician visit. Some have already tried a lot and want to know what else has been studied.

**Mission (Phase 4):** Teach the calibrated obesity landscape faithfully. Reduce the work the reader has to do, without removing scientific honesty or the relationships the evidence uncovered.

We are not trying to impress readers with the evidence. We are trying to equip them to use it, with their clinician's input.

## What this product is not

- Not a clinical guideline or algorithm that chooses for the reader  
- Not a prescribing monograph, compounding protocol, or dosing guide  
- Not an advocacy document for lifestyle over medicines (or the reverse)  
- Not a skeptical takedown of diets, exercise, programmes, surgery, or supplements  
- Not a top-three shortlist for people who have “tried everything”  
- Not a report card that grades treatments A through F  
- Not a generic “weight-loss tips” article that could live on any site  
- Not editorial prose in the Insights voice  
- Not a claim that every kilogram, waist, or composition change equals proven protection from heart attack, stroke, or death  
- Not a place that quietly turns medicines into background wallpaper behind lifestyle advice  
- Not a combined “lifestyle” page that collapses named diets, programmes, and exercise  
- Not a combined “shots vs surgery” page  
- Not a type 2 diabetes remission map (no DiRECT import; no two reversal routes)  
- Not an Emerging Therapies peptide studio folio

## The one-map decision (obesity's defining shape)

Phase 3 established **one Obesity hub with six intervention topic pages**. Broad clinical obesity as people search it is the spine.

That architecture is **earned**, not imposed. The six frozen objects resolve into one shared decision landscape. The important divergences are internal claim types and annotations, not sufficiently independent treatment universes to justify BMI-class hubs, pediatric hubs, a shots-vs-surgery split, or a merge with type 2 diabetes.

Six topic pages match six frozen objects. That count is earned from this campaign. It is not copied from type 2 diabetes’s six pages, which taught two remission routes this map does not have.

Material differences appear as:

- **Hub annotations** (weight ≠ events; weight ranking ≠ MACE ranking; SELECT does not cover everyone; surgery ≠ endoscopy; programmes ≠ named diets)  
- **Topic self-identification** where needed (especially medicines and procedures)  
- **Non-collapse of already-separate objects** (eating pattern ≠ programme delivery ≠ exercise; surgery and endoscopy stay on one page as two branches)

This is the central design constraint for Phase 4:

- Welcome readers who only have the words “obesity” or “weight loss.”  
- Keep obesity medicines on the map, not a silent backdrop.  
- Keep the medicines divider visible: losing more weight is not the same as better heart protection, and SELECT does not apply to everyone with obesity.  
- Keep surgery and endoscopy on one procedures page as two different options.  
- Keep eating patterns, behavioural programmes, and exercise as three pages. No combined lifestyle page.  
- Keep popular supplements as an honest investigated page without promotion.  
- Teach once, as map grammar: a change in weight is not the same as proven protection from heart attack, stroke, or death; the medicine that loses the most weight is not automatically the one with heart-outcome evidence.  
- Translate internal calibration into reader-facing roles without a letter-grade reading.  
- **A topic page is not evidentiary parity.** Six pages means six decisions worth teaching. Hierarchy lives in section order, space, and Bottom lines, not in equal visual weight.

## Teaching constraint · no report-card bands

**Internal only:** Phase 3 letters A / B / C / D / E / F.

Those letters name landscape **bands** (pharmacologic weight/harm with a population-bound heart-outcome warrant inside one medicine; long-term procedural weight with two branches; pattern-level weight/risk-factor gradient with fade; composition/fitness map with three jobs; investigated behavioural delivery; investigated supplement shelf). Prefer “bands” or “roles” over “tiers” in writer notes. They are **not** treatment grades, popularity ranks, or “A is best / F is worst” scores.

**There is no A′ on this map.** Hypertension used a prime for a parallel hard-outcome product. Type 2 diabetes correctly refused A′ for surgery. Obesity refuses it twice: SELECT is internal to one medicine and one enrolled population; SOS mortality is observational, not a randomized heart-outcome trial. Calling either A′ would misidentify the kind of evidence it earned.

**There are not two remission routes.** Type 2 diabetes needed two pages for surgery versus a formula-diet remission program. This campaign does not. Behavioural programmes are not DiRECT. VLCD stays on the eating-patterns page. Do not print a “reversal” pair onto obesity because the sibling map had one.

**Public pages must not:**

- Print A, B, C, D, E, or F as reader-facing labels, chips, or At a Glance keys  
- Invent an A′ for SELECT or for surgery  
- Imply programmes or supplements are weaker versions of “real” care, or that medicines are optional wallpaper  
- Imply “investigated” means “disproven”  
- Imply six topic pages are six equal treatments  
- Group eating patterns, programmes, and exercise so they read as one lifestyle option  

**Public pages must:**

- Use role language (see writers key below)  
- Encode hierarchy through section order, space, and plain-language Bottom lines  
- Recover the Phase 3 map from roles and relationships, not from letter grades  
- Put weight ≠ events and weight ranking ≠ MACE ranking on the hub before topic pages  

Writer notes and fidelity tables in drafts may still mention internal letters. HTML body copy, headings, At a Glance, and question index must not.

### Public roles table (writers key)

| Internal (writers only) | Public role language |
|---|---|
| A | Obesity medicines (how much weight they lose is not the same ranking as heart-attack evidence; one medicine has heart-outcome evidence in people who already have heart disease) |
| B | Surgery and endoscopy (surgery has stronger long-term weight evidence among procedures; endoscopy is a different option, not gentler surgery; observational survival data are not the same as a randomized heart-outcome trial) |
| C | Eating patterns (named diets compared for weight; much of the difference fades by a year; formula diets are part of this page, not a separate reversal program) |
| D | Exercise (modest average weight change; different jobs for fat loss, keeping muscle, and fitness) |

| F | Popular supplements (what was studied; small or not clinically important weight change; not proven event prevention) |

Do not print the left column on public pages. Refine ordinary wording in hub/topic drafts as needed, without changing relative place.

## Teaching constraint · landscape grammar (weight ≠ events; weight ranking ≠ MACE ranking)

Teach once on the hub, **before** readers enter topic pages, and keep visible on every weight, composition, or programme topic page:

**A change in weight, waist, or body composition is not the same as proven protection from heart attack, stroke, or death.**

**The medicine that loses the most weight is not automatically the one with heart-outcome evidence. Heart-outcome evidence from one medicine in people who already have heart disease does not cover every anti-obesity medicine, and it does not cover everyone with obesity.**

Some options on this map have selected hard-outcome evidence (subcutaneous semaglutide 2.4 mg in people with preexisting cardiovascular disease, BMI ≥27, and no diabetes). Others mainly show weight, fat, lean mass, fitness, or programme-delivery change. Surgery’s strongest long-term claim among procedures is greater weight loss, not a randomized heart-outcome trial. Public copy must not let kilograms stand in for events, or let a one-year weight ranking stand in for heart protection, where the frozen claim does not support that leap.

This grammar also explains why tirzepatide can lead one-year weight change without inheriting SELECT; why aerobic training’s modest average is not “exercise does not matter”; and why a supplement that moves weight a little does not automatically inherit event prevention.

## Teaching constraint · non-collapse locks (public terms)

| Lock | Public meaning |
|---|---|
| Weight ranking ≠ heart-outcome ranking | Tirzepatide led one-year weight loss in the comparison studied. Subcutaneous semaglutide is the medicine with heart-attack and death evidence in high-risk people. Do not crown the bigger weight loss as the better heart medicine. |
| SELECT ≠ everyone with obesity | That trial enrolled people aged 45 and older with preexisting heart disease, BMI ≥27, and no diabetes, using semaglutide 2.4 mg. It does not cover primary-prevention obesity or every incretin. |
| Compounded products are a warning, not a protocol | Unregulated compounded pens are not an option to optimize on this page. |
| Investigational neighbors stay unnamed as treatments | Later-generation shots discussed in news or studio pages are watches here, not promoted options. |
| Eating patterns ≠ programmes | Choosing Atkins, DASH, or Mediterranean is not the same as joining a structured weight-management program. |
| Formula diets ≠ DiRECT | Very-low-calorie or formula protocols live on the eating-patterns page. They are not a type 2 diabetes remission program imported from the other map. |
| Meal replacements in a program ≠ formula-diet page | A program that uses meal replacements is still a program. Do not let that swallow the formula-diet discussion, or the reverse. |
| 6-month diet ranks ≠ 12-month winners | Named-diet differences at six months are small. Weight loss diminished at 12 months. Fade is part of the claim. |
| Vegetarian presence ≠ vegan invention | Vegetarian and Ornish-class programs were studied. Unstudied vegan-only plates do not inherit Mediterranean evidence. |
| Exercise jobs ≠ kilogram contest | Aerobic training for modest fat loss, resistance training to help keep muscle during weight loss, and any mode for fitness are three jobs. Modest average weight is not irrelevance and not a rival to medicines. |
| Regain and lasting difference travel together | After a program ends, weight often returns faster. A difference versus usual care can still last for years. Teach both. Do not pick the cleaner half. |
| Surgery ≠ endoscopy | Endoscopy is not gentler surgery. Except for endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty, endoscopic procedures were less effective than newer medicines in the comparison studied. Long-term endoscopic data are missing. |
| Short-term shot vs surgery ≠ merger | Some medicines looked similar to some operations at about one year. That does not retire long-term surgery, and it does not put shots and surgery on one page. |
| Observational surgery survival ≠ randomized heart-outcome trial | Long-term observational survival after surgery is not the same kind of evidence as SELECT. |
| Supplements ≠ promotion | Investigated after looking. Small or not clinically important weight signals. No named product promotion. |
| Not the diabetes map | Overlap is real. This is not the type 2 diabetes page. Heart-outcome evidence without diabetes lives here. Formula-diet diabetes remission lives there. |

Cross-links between medicines ↔ procedures, eating patterns ↔ programmes, eating patterns ↔ exercise, and medicines ↔ the rest of the map are required. Medicines must mention lifestyle and procedures without disappearing into them.

## Educational Principle (condition application)

From the product charter: the purpose is not the shortest framework or cleanest taxonomy. It is to educate and empower the reader. The evidence determines what deserves attention, how much space it receives, and how it is explained. We do not simplify by omitting nuance that changes understanding.

For obesity, that means:

| Topic | Why space expands or contracts |
|---|---|
| Obesity medicines | Highest first-decision stakes; weight ranking vs heart-outcome ranking, SELECT population, older agents, investigational watches, and compounded-product warning coexist inside one object |
| Surgery and endoscopy | Four tensions on one page (long-term surgery, distinct endoscopy, short-term medicine comparison, observational survival); prevents “shots replaced surgery” and “endoscopy is surgery-lite” |
| Eating patterns | 12-month fade and formula-diet branch must travel with six-month ranks; stop “best diet” and DiRECT import |
| Exercise | Three jobs; stop sneer (only a few kilograms) and upgrade (events or rival to shots) |
| Behavioral programmes | Dual durability finding; stop “programmes fail” and merger with named diets; page stays shorter than medicines |
| Popular supplements | Desperate-reader coverage without rewarding search popularity with evidentiary prominence, and without erasing the page because the evidence is small |

**A topic page is not a claim of equal weight.** Programmes and supplements still get pages because someone is actually considering them. Those pages stay shorter and do not imitate the medicines page.

**Pressure-test:** Prefer “Would a patient, family member, clinician, or student leave with a faithful map?” over “Can this be shorter?”

**Desperate-reader test:** Someone who has tried many treatments and is searching “what else?” deserves the full honest landscape of what was studied, what each option can support, and where it fits, not a top-three shortlist.

## Plant-based honesty

Vegetarian and Ornish-class programs are on the eating-patterns map. Do not invent unstudied vegan-only patterns from Mediterranean evidence. Do not treat Mediterranean as the only pattern that could work if others were studied. For supplements, plant-based marketing does not upgrade a small trial. Green tea is not a responsible substitute for medicines. Report analogues honestly when an animal-derived product surfaces; do not invent transfer.

## Language rules

**Reduce cognitive load.** Same claims; simpler sentences; less decoding.

| Editorial habit | This page |
|---|---|
| “Pharmacologic comparative weight/harm spine plus a population-bound MACE warrant inside one agent.” | “Obesity medicines differ in how much weight they help people lose. Losing more weight is not the same as better evidence for preventing heart attacks. One medicine has that heart-outcome evidence in people who already have heart disease.” |
| “No A′; SOS observational, not SELECT-class.” | “Long-term studies of surgery include observational survival findings. Those are not the same kind of evidence as a randomized heart-outcome trial of a medicine.” |
| “6- vs 12-month fade; VLCD internal.” | “Named diets look more different at six months than at a year. Very-low-calorie or formula diets are a different protocol on this same page, not a diabetes-remission program.” |
| “Three jobs; modest weight is not irrelevance.” | “Exercise usually changes average weight only a little. It still has separate jobs: losing some fat, helping keep muscle while you lose weight, and improving fitness.” |
| “Regain and maintained between-group difference as one grain.” | “After a program ends, weight often comes back faster. People who joined a program can still weigh less than people who didn't, years later. Both parts of that finding matter.” |

**Every extra word should buy information, not style.**

Good uses of length: what the option is; what participation usually asks; who was studied; realistic expectations; burden and access; what to ask a clinician.

Bad uses of length: extra framing paragraphs, elegant transitions, methodology the reader does not need, nuance theater that changes no decision, letter-grade explanations, construction notes about why we did not merge pages.

**Intervention reality rule:** Do not explain only the verdict. Give enough concrete reality to picture the intervention and make the verdict meaningful.

**Equal honesty does not mean equal length.**

**Contractions by default. No em dashes.** Project voice policy. Beat rhythm on openings: short permission/affirmation/pivot beats get their own lines when writing HTML later.

**Don't make the reader learn our framework in order to learn the medicine.** Teach the medicine clearly enough that the framework becomes apparent on its own. Hub and topic copy must not leak internal vocabulary such as “seat,” “rail,” “warrant,” “earns,” “grain,” “node,” “placement,” “inflate,” “shelf with dividers,” “what the evidence earns,” or band letters A–F.

Useful clinical words may stay **after** the page has taught them (GLP-1, tirzepatide, endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty, very-low-calorie diet). Metaphors that help the reader may stay. Metaphors that only help us must go.

Follow [`.cursor/rules/intervention-maps-public-language.mdc`](../../.cursor/rules/intervention-maps-public-language.mdc). Evidence heading: **What the evidence shows**. Combined heading: **Who was studied, expectations, and limits**.

## Awareness color

**Family locked August 13, 2026. Hex locked:** `#96720a` / ink `#684e08`. Design identity, not an official obesity-awareness color.

- **Family:** yellow → restrained mustard/gold. Informed by common obesity-awareness association. Not an official ribbon designation.  
- **Not a claim** that yellow is “the obesity ribbon,” or that any governing obesity organization owns this color.  
- **World Obesity Day 2026 blue/white** has the stronger organizational pedigree. It is unused because it would make obesity visually crowd type 2 diabetes (`#2f6499`). Campaign branding, not a ribbon claim.  
- **Older WOF mustard accent** (`#EBAF00` in a 2022 brand book) is useful corroboration that mustard is not visually alien to obesity advocacy. Do not promote it into an official designation, and do not copy that hex onto Intervention Maps paper.  
- **Sibling collisions:** ADHD already occupies orange (`#e87722`). Type 2 diabetes occupies IDF-family blue (`#2f6499`). Hypertension and hyperlipidemia occupy cardiovascular reds.  
- **Avoid lemon yellow** on `#e4e8ec` paper (weak contrast; ribbon-shop look).  
- **Public language:** do not describe this family as gold in any public-facing awareness explanation (childhood-cancer gold ribbon). Internally, “mustard/gold” is a color description only.  
- **Hex:** `#96720a` / `#684e08`. Lighter than the first hub candidate, still mustard rather than lemon or childhood-cancer gold. Chosen on Intervention Maps paper for contrast, not by copying a ribbon-shop yellow. Do not use World Obesity Federation or OAC marks.

Do not recolor the evidence landscape. Do not turn condition color into decoration.

## Success test

A concerned, less research-savvy reader can:

1. Recover the calibrated map from roles and section order without reading investigations, and without seeing letter grades.  
2. State that obesity medicines are on the map, not hidden behind lifestyle tips.  
3. Tell one-year weight rankings apart from heart-outcome evidence, and know that SELECT does not cover everyone with obesity.  
4. Tell surgery apart from endoscopy, and know they share one page because they are related procedure decisions, not because they are the same treatment.  
5. State that a change in weight is not automatically the same as proven event protection.  
6. Know that named diets look more alike by a year than at six months, and that formula diets are a different protocol on that page, not a diabetes-remission program.  
7. Know that exercise’s modest average weight change does not erase muscle and fitness jobs.  
8. Know that after a program ends, weight often returns, and a difference versus usual care can still last; joining a program is not choosing a named diet.  
9. Know that popular supplements were investigated without being sold as proven weight-loss or event prevention, and without thinking a supplements page means they sit beside medicines.  
10. Leave with specific questions for their clinician.  
11. If they have already tried several treatments, still find an honest map of what else has been studied, without being sold a miracle or dismissed.

If they also enjoyed the prose, fine. That is not the goal.
