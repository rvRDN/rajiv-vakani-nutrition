# Obesity Evidence Library v0.1

**Status:** Phase 0 minimal library **owner-accepted and frozen** as canonical v0.1 (8 sources)  
**Date:** August 13, 2026  
**Role:** Canonical membership, roles, and rationale for the obesity evidence library. Not a summary. Not an article draft.  
**Research object:** Obesity (broad clinical obesity as people search it). Lifestyle foundations, anti-obesity medicines, behavioral programs, surgery, devices, supplements, herbs/CAM, and popular home-remedy claims are all in scope for investigation. Hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and type 2 diabetes remain sibling campaigns. The Emerging Therapies peptide studio is a different product.  
**Principle:** Completeness with minimal redundancy. Prefer primary sources. Preserve controversies. Currentness through **13 August 2026** is a membership test, not a preference.  
**Companions:** [`README.md`](README.md) · [`citation-log-v0.1.md`](citation-log-v0.1.md) · [`CHANGELOG.md`](CHANGELOG.md) · [`proposals/`](proposals/) · [`obesity-campaign-brief-v0.1.md`](obesity-campaign-brief-v0.1.md)

**Maintenance rule:** Do not silently add or remove accepted sources. Propose revisions in `proposals/`.

**Scope note vs Brain batch:** Lifestyle and anti-obesity medicines are on-map evidence, not comparator-only backdrop. That follows the obesity decision problem (same claim-calibration method; different research-object breadth).

**Metadata rule:** Every seated source carries **Population** (adults with overweight or obesity / mixed comorbidity / established CVD without diabetes / surgery-eligible), **Weight / health context** (BMI, waist, percent weight loss, body composition, comorbidities), and **Claim type** (weight change, risk-factor change, hard cardiovascular outcomes, mixed). This is search hygiene, not architecture. Do not smear a kilogram-loss warrant onto heart attack, stroke, or death claims without the source earning it.

**Plant-based rule:** When an animal-derived intervention is on the map, check the plant-based analogue readers will ask about when appropriate. Report what exists or that it does not. Do not invent claim transfer. Owner nutrition curiosity does **not** lower the membership bar; diet and plant-based pattern sources earn seats only when they survive the same pressure-test as medicines.

## Pressure-test mandate

Assume a skeptical systematic reviewer.

For every candidate source, ask:

1. Does a stronger or more recent source already cover the same material?  
2. If removed, does any intervention domain, population boundary, landmark trial, or major controversy disappear?  
3. Is it foundational, comprehensive, current, or uniquely informative?  
4. Does it help answer where an intervention fits, rather than merely show that someone studied it?  
5. Does weight measure, comorbidity context, or guideline era materially alter the landscape?  
6. Does it distinguish weight / risk-factor change from hard cardiovascular outcomes when the claim requires it?

Only sources that survive that test enter the **minimal permanent library**.

### A. Standard-of-care anchors

#### A1. AACE Consensus Statement: Algorithm for the Evaluation and Treatment of Adults with Obesity/Adiposity-Based Chronic Disease, 2025 Update (Nadolsky, Garvey, et al.)
- **Population:** Adults with obesity / adiposity-based chronic disease; pediatrics out of scope  
- **Weight / health context:** Anthropometric plus clinical (complication) diagnosis; weight-loss targets tied to complications, not BMI alone  
- **Claim type:** Mixed (lifestyle, medication hierarchies, complication-centric goals)  
- **Why it belongs:** Current U.S. SOC algorithm. Moves the map off “BMI first” toward complication-centric care. Carries lifestyle and FDA-approved medicines. Surgery is deliberately out of scope, so C3 and A3 must carry that domain.  
- **Controversy preserved:** Complication-centric U.S. algorithm vs NICE BMI/waist-to-height pathway vs Obesity Canada ethnicity-specific BMI plus central adiposity to start medicines.

#### A2. Pedersen et al., 2025. Pharmacotherapy for obesity management in adults: 2025 clinical practice guideline update. *CMAJ*
- **Population:** Adults with excess adiposity; BMI ≥30 or ≥27 with adiposity-related complications (ethnicity-specific interpretation)  
- **Weight / health context:** Central adiposity plus complications; long-term pharmacotherapy; subpopulations (ASCVD, HFpEF, MASH, OSA, OA, prediabetes, T2D, monogenic)  
- **Claim type:** Mixed (weight, maintenance, selected hard and complication outcomes)  
- **Why it belongs:** Current GRADE pharmacotherapy CPG. Unique among the A set for an explicit semaglutide 2.4 mg MACE recommendation in established ASCVD, a long-term-use/maintenance stance, and a recommendation against compounded products. 2020 Obesity Canada CPG remains companion for behaviour and surgery chapters.  
- **Controversy preserved:** Long-term pharmacotherapy vs “a course of shots”; SELECT-eligible ASCVD population vs everyone with obesity; labeled medicines vs compounded peptides.

#### A3. NICE NG246. Overweight and obesity management
- **Population:** Children, young people, and adults with overweight, obesity, or central adiposity; pregnancy excluded  
- **Weight / health context:** BMI plus waist-to-height ratio; behavioural services; dietary advice; physical activity; medicines; surgical referral  
- **Claim type:** Mixed  
- **Why it belongs:** Current UK full pathway (last reviewed 8 January 2026). The only A-set document that carries behavioural services, surgery referral, and children on one map. Prevents A1/A2 from turning Phase 0 into a medicines-only library.  
- **Controversy preserved:** UK service-pathway language vs U.S. complication-centric algorithm vs Canadian GRADE drug recs; BMI class vs central adiposity; children remain a watch-list rail, not a Step 0 split.

### B. Landscape syntheses

#### B1. Nong et al., 2026. Comparative effects of drugs for adults with overweight or obesity: systematic review and network meta-analysis. *BMJ*
- **Population:** Adults with overweight or obesity, with or without cardiometabolic complications; 262 RCTs; 99,791 participants; 19 drugs  
- **Weight / health context:** Percent weight at one year; fat and lean mass; GI harms and discontinuation; all-cause mortality; MI; heart failure; kidney failure; quality of life  
- **Claim type:** Mixed. Weight and harms are GRADE-rated. Hard-outcome estimates are largely informed by high-risk CVOTs and must not be smeared onto every drug or every person with obesity. Search and publication through **8 July 2026**.  
- **Why it belongs:** Comparative medicine landscape the A set pathways do not fully quantify. Tirzepatide and CagriSema largest one-year weight effects (moderate to high certainty); subcutaneous semaglutide uniquely associated with lower all-cause mortality and MI in this package; tirzepatide also reduced fat mass the most and lean mass the most. Emerging agents (retatrutide, orforglipron, and neighbors) appear at low or very low certainty. That is the Emerging Therapies boundary inside the library, not a merge with the studio.  
- **Controversy preserved:** Greatest weight loss ≠ proven mortality/MI benefit; greater lean-mass loss is part of the tirzepatide package; investigational agents are not approved-medicine warrants; no drug convincingly improved quality of life beyond a small minimally important difference.

### C. Domain-defining syntheses

#### C1. Ge et al., 2020. Comparison of dietary macronutrient patterns of 14 popular named dietary programmes… *BMJ*
- **Population:** Overweight or obese adults; 121 RCTs; 21,942 participants; 14 named programmes plus three control diets  
- **Weight / health context:** Weight and cardiovascular risk factors at 6 and 12 months  
- **Claim type:** Weight and risk-factor change; **not** hard CV events  
- **Why it belongs:** Current high-bar comparative eating-pattern map. Low-carbohydrate and low-fat similar at six months; named-diet differences small; weight loss diminished at 12 months; risk-factor benefits largely disappeared except Mediterranean. Vegetarian/Ornish-class programmes sit on the map rather than being invented from Mediterranean evidence.  
- **Controversy preserved:** Named-diet ranking at six months ≠ a 12-month winner and ≠ proven MI/stroke reduction; adherence, not brand, is the practical grain; vegan-only claims are not this network.

#### C2. Oppert, Bellicha, van Baak, et al., 2021. Exercise training in the management of overweight and obesity in adults. *Obesity Reviews* (EASO Physical Activity Working Group)
- **Population:** Adults with overweight or obesity; synthesis of seven EASO systematic reviews  
- **Weight / health context:** Weight, total and visceral fat, lean mass, fitness, insulin sensitivity, blood pressure, quality of life  
- **Claim type:** Weight and composition/fitness change; **not** hard CV events  
- **Why it belongs:** Domain-defining exercise map. Aerobic training preferentially advised for weight and fat (average not more than 2–3 kg); resistance training for lean-mass preservation during weight loss; any mode for fitness and insulin sensitivity. Keeps exercise on the map without promoting it to a weight-loss rival of incretins or surgery. Bellicha 2021 remains companion for the 12-review / 149-study overview.  
- **Controversy preserved:** Exercise-mode ranking ≠ hard-outcome ranking; modest mean weight is not “exercise does not matter”; lean-mass preservation is a different job from kilogram loss.

#### C3. De Luca, Cohen, Belluzzi, et al., 2026. Efficacy and safety of pharmacological, endoscopic, and surgical treatments for obesity: a GRADE-based network meta-analysis. *Obesity*
- **Population:** Adults with obesity; 139 RCTs (54 surgery, 21 endoscopic, 64 medicines); search to **1 December 2024**  
- **Weight / health context:** Percent total body weight loss at 26–52, 53–104, 105–156, and ≥156 weeks; serious adverse events  
- **Claim type:** Weight over time plus SAE; **not** a SELECT-class CVOT  
- **Why it belongs:** Owner-relevant crowded-landscape test: surgery and endoscopic procedures would disappear if left as named gaps while medicines have B1. Metabolic surgery appears superior long term (RYGB, sleeve, SADI, BPD). Endoscopic procedures except ESG were less effective than newer medicines. Semaglutide and tirzepatide showed no inferior short-term weight results vs some surgery. Long-term data lacking for most medicines and all endoscopic procedures.  
- **Controversy preserved:** Short-term incretin ≈ some surgery ≠ long-term surgery; endoscopic ≠ surgery; SAE costs (highest long-term with BPD) are part of the decision; SOS observational mortality stays companion, not a clean CVOT warrant.

### D. Landmark trials

#### D1. Lincoff et al., 2023. Semaglutide and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Obesity without Diabetes (SELECT). *NEJM*
- **Population:** Age ≥45 years; BMI ≥27; preexisting CVD; no diabetes; 17,604 randomized  
- **Weight / health context:** Semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly vs placebo, added to CVD standard of care; mean follow-up 39.8 months  
- **Claim type:** Hard outcomes (3-point MACE; HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.72–0.90)  
- **Why it belongs:** Landmark obesity-without-diabetes CVOT. Type 2 diabetes left this trial outside that map on purpose. B1 uses CVOT-informed mortality/MI estimates; SELECT uniquely carries the public warrant that an obesity-dose incretin reduced MACE in people without diabetes. Discontinuation for adverse events was higher with semaglutide (16.6% vs 8.2%).  
- **Controversy preserved:** Established-CVD enrollment ≠ everyone with obesity; MACE reduction is not proven for tirzepatide, older medicines, diet, or exercise from this trial; SURMOUNT-MMO is not a result yet; weight change in SELECT is not the same claim as MACE.

## Explicit gaps (not seats)

Keep visible. Do not invent Phase 0 seats to fill them.

1. **Green tea, Garcinia, CLA, chromium, and similar “fat-burner” products** — Small or non-significant weight signals; no hard-outcome package that earns a C.  
2. **Ayurvedic and other CAM products** — Prior Insights investigation is a different product; options remain in scope for later objects, not imported as a pre-calibrated ladder.  
3. **Behavioural weight-management programmes as a standalone C** — A3 carries the pathway; working evidence later if an object demands it.  
4. **VLCD / formula diets as a forced C** — Placement in A3; not DiRECT imported from the type 2 diabetes campaign.  
5. **Investigational incretins as approved-medicine seats** — B1 reports retatrutide, orforglipron, CagriSema, and neighbors at low or mixed certainty. Emerging Therapies studio remains the molecule-level room. Do not import studio claim ladders.  
6. **SURMOUNT-MMO and other unfinished obesity-without-diabetes CVOTs** — Named watch, not a second D.  
7. **Pediatric / adolescent obesity as a rail** — A3 includes children; architecture waits for Phase 2/3.  
8. **Algae-oil or other plant-based analogues of animal-derived products** — Check when an animal-derived option surfaces (whey, collagen, dairy-heavy patterns). Vegetarian/Ornish-class patterns are already inside C1; do not invent unstudied vegan patterns from Mediterranean evidence.  
9. **Compounded or unregulated peptide products** — A2 recommends against them; that is a safety-context finding, not a C.

## Controversies the library must preserve

1. **Weight / waist / composition change ≠ heart attack, stroke, or death.** C1/C2/C3 weight warrants are not D1 hard-outcome warrants. B1’s weight ranking is not its mortality/MI ranking.  
2. **Durability and regain are part of the claim.** Ge’s 12-month fade, A2’s long-term pharmacotherapy stance, and C3’s missing long-term medicine/endoscopic data are the same grain.  
3. **Greatest weight loss is not automatically greatest cardiovascular protection.** Tirzepatide/CagriSema lead B1’s one-year weight network; subcutaneous semaglutide carries the SELECT-informed MACE/mortality signal.  
4. **SELECT’s population is established CVD without diabetes.** Do not export that MACE result to primary-prevention obesity, to tirzepatide, or to lifestyle programmes.  
5. **Surgery’s long-term weight advantage is not erased by short-term incretin non-inferiority**, and incretin SAE profiles are not surgery SAE profiles.  
6. **U.S. / Canadian / UK SOC disagrees in culture** (A1–A3): complication-centric algorithm vs GRADE drug recs vs service pathway, including children.  
7. **Exercise’s job is not “lose as much as a shot.”** Modest mean weight sits beside composition, fitness, and lean-mass preservation.  
8. **Do not merge this map with type 2 diabetes.** Dual agonists stay investigable here for the obesity decision problem; T2D remission/DiRECT/SURPASS-CVOT architecture stays on that map.  
9. **Do not import the Emerging Therapies studio as this campaign’s architecture.** Overlapping molecules are expected.  
10. **Plant-based eating is a named-programme presence in C1, not a transferred Mediterranean warrant.**
