## Editorial principles

1. **Lead with the map, not the aisle.** Public heat for “best diet,” brand-name shots, sleeve vs bypass, or green-tea fat burners is not placement.  
2. **One shared hub; annotations, not extra hubs.** BMI class, age, and diabetes overlap annotate. They do not multiply hubs.  
3. **Claim language over hype language.** Prefer the freeze-register sentence or a plain-language twin that is not stronger.  
4. **Weight ≠ events, and weight ranking ≠ MACE ranking, are landscape grammar.** Teach on the hub **before** topic links; keep visible on weight, composition, and programme topic pages.  
5. **Medicines stay on the map.** Not lifestyle backdrop.  
6. **Space follows understanding.** Medicines and procedures may run longer where misunderstanding risk is high. Programme and supplement pages stay compact. A topic page is not evidentiary parity.  
7. **No public band letters.** Internal A / B / C / D / E / F stay in drafts only. Public hierarchy uses **roles**. There is no A′. There is no imported diabetes remission pair.  
8. **No combined lifestyle page. No combined shots-vs-surgery page.** Eating patterns, programmes, and exercise are three pages. Procedures is one page with two branches.  
9. **If a heading fights the frozen map,** fix the outline, not the frozen objects, unless a sentence would exceed §12.

## Page skeleton (condition hub)

### 0. Title / intent (above the fold)

- **Working title:** Obesity: where each option sits in the evidence  
- **Welcome beats (separate short `<p>`s when writing HTML; beat-rhythm rule):**  
  - If you're trying to understand your options for obesity or weight loss, including after several approaches haven't been enough, you're in the right place.  
  - That's a fair reason to be here.  
- **Page claim:** This map shows obesity medicines, surgery and endoscopy, eating patterns, exercise, behavioral programs, and popular supplements as different kinds of decisions. A change in weight is not automatically the same as proven protection from heart attack, stroke, or death. The medicine that helps people lose the most weight is not automatically the one with heart-outcome evidence. Heart-outcome evidence from one medicine in people who already have heart disease does not cover every shot, and it does not cover everyone with obesity.  
- **Safeguard:** Use this map to understand the options and prepare better questions for your clinician. It isn't a guide to starting, stopping, or changing treatment on your own.

Do not use “seat,” “rail,” “band,” or A–F letters in the lede.

- Bring questions to a clinician; do not self-replace indicated care.

**Length budget:** Short. Not a methodology essay. Do not explain A–F letters. Prefer ordinary language.

### 3. Short group orientation (hub, then topics)

#### 3a. Obesity medicines

**Topic page:** `obesity-medicines.html`  
**Slug / folder:** `obesity-medicines`  
**Public title (working):** Obesity medicines  
**Internal calibration:** A

- Comparative one-year weight and harm first (tirzepatide led weight loss in the comparison studied; subcutaneous semaglutide and older approved agents remain named).  
- Weight ranking ≠ heart-outcome ranking in the first teaching movement.  
- SELECT: semaglutide 2.4 mg in people with preexisting heart disease, BMI ≥27, no diabetes; major heart events reduced. Population-bound. Not every incretin. Not everyone with obesity.  
- Older approved agents stay visible (access is part of the desperate-reader test).  
- Investigational neighbors stay watches, not promoted treatments.  
- Compounded products: warning, not a protocol.  
- U.S. / Canadian / NICE culture disagreement taught in plain language, not silently won.  
- On-map, not lifestyle backdrop. Educational pathways, not a formulary.

**Shape on hub:** Short orientation + link. Full teaching on the topic page.

#### 3b. Surgery and endoscopy

**Topic page:** `obesity-surgery-and-endoscopy.html`  
**Slug / folder:** `metabolic-and-endoscopic-procedures`  
**Public title (working):** Surgery and endoscopy  
**Internal calibration:** B (not A′; not a weaker medicine grade)

- Long-term surgical weight advantage among procedures first (bypass, sleeve, and related operations as internal types).  
- Endoscopy is a different option, not gentler surgery. Except for endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty, endoscopic procedures were less effective than newer medicines in the comparison studied. Long-term endoscopic data are missing.  
- Some medicines looked similar to some operations at about one year. That annotates; it does not merge this page with medicines and does not retire long-term surgery.  
- Serious adverse events are higher with procedures than with medicines; the most malabsorptive operations carry the highest long-term harm signal in this package.  
- Observational long-term survival after surgery is not the same kind of evidence as a randomized heart-outcome trial.  
- Required cross-link to obesity medicines.

#### 3c. Eating patterns

**Topic page:** `obesity-eating-patterns.html`  
**Slug / folder:** `eating-patterns`  
**Public title (working):** Eating patterns  
**Internal calibration:** C

- Comparative weight/risk-factor gradient: low-carbohydrate and low-fat similar at six months; named-diet differences small.  
- 12-month fade travels with every six-month comparison. Not “the best obesity diet.” Not “diets do not work.”  
- Ranking is weight and risk factors, not events. Vegetarian/Ornish-class programs stay on the map. Unstudied vegan-only plates do not inherit Mediterranean evidence.  
- Formula / very-low-calorie diets are a mandatory internal section (extra loss at one year versus a program alone, smaller later). Not DiRECT. Not a seventh page.  
- Required cross-links to behavioral programs and exercise.

#### 3d. Exercise

**Topic page:** `obesity-exercise.html`  
**Slug / folder:** `exercise`  
**Public title (working):** Exercise  
**Internal calibration:** D

- Three jobs: aerobic training for modest weight and fat (average not more than about 2–3 kg); resistance training to help keep muscle during weight loss; any mode for fitness and insulin sensitivity.  
- Modest average weight is not “exercise does not matter.” It is not a rival to medicines.  
- Ranking is composition and fitness, not events. Modes stay internal. Do not build a modality tourism page.  
- Required cross-link to eating patterns.

#### 3e. Behavioral weight-management programs

**Topic page:** `obesity-behavioural-programmes.html`  
**Slug / folder:** `behavioural-programmes`  
**Public title (working):** Behavioral weight-management programs  
**Internal calibration:** E

- Joining a structured program is not choosing a named diet and not choosing an exercise mode.  
- Dual finding: after a program ends, weight often returns faster; a difference versus usual care can still last for years. Teach both.  
- Primary-care packages: about 2 kg extra at 12 months versus comparator in the working evidence. Report the number. Do not convert it into events.  
- Named commercial brands do not earn distinct promotion.  
- Meal replacements as a program component must not swallow the formula-diet section on eating patterns.  
- A page here does not mean evidentiary parity with medicines. Do not imitate the medicines page’s length.  
- Required cross-link to eating patterns.

#### 3f. Popular supplements

**Topic page:** `obesity-popular-supplements.html`  
**Slug / folder:** `popular-supplements`  
**Public title (working):** Popular supplements  
**Internal calibration:** F

- Investigated after looking. Closed promotion gate is the finding.  
- Small or not clinically important weight signals (green tea; Garcinia and related herbals). No heart-outcome evidence.  
- No named product promotion. Ayurvedic products stay named branches, not an imported Insights ladder.  
- A page here does not mean evidentiary parity with medicines. Do not imitate the medicines or diet pages.

### 4. Closing / how to read uncertainty

- This page orients the landscape; it does not replace personal clinical care.  
- If you have already tried many options, use the index to find what else has been studied and what each option can actually support.  
- No letter grades; different options do different jobs. Six pages are not six equal treatments.

## Hub question index

Group questions by the six destinations, in the same order as At a Glance (not alphabet and not A–F):

1. **Obesity medicines:** Do shots replace diet and surgery? Which medicine loses the most weight, and does that mean it protects the heart? Does heart-outcome evidence cover every GLP-1 and everyone with obesity? Are compounded pens the same as labeled medicines?  
2. **Surgery and endoscopy:** Is endoscopy a gentler surgery? Did shots replace operations? Does surgery prevent heart attacks?  
3. **Eating patterns:** Which diet is best, and do the kilos last? Are formula or very-low-calorie diets a different decision? Does plant-based mean one plate?  
4. **Exercise:** Does exercise even matter if the average is only a few kilograms? Which kind of training, and is that about heart attacks?  
5. **Behavioral programs:** Do commercial or clinic programs work, or does the weight just come back? Is joining a program the same as choosing a named diet?  
6. **Popular supplements:** Do green tea, Garcinia, or “fat burners” replace care?

Every index item links to a written topic answer. Completeness of inventory matters more than equal length.

## Topic page floor (shared Intervention Maps grammar)

1. Bottom line matching §12 (plain language; not stronger)  
2. Decision-relevant sections shaped by the object (question grammar: what it is; participation; common confusions; what the evidence shows; who was studied / expectations / limits; what to ask)  
3. Ask your clinician box  
4. Collapsed Evidence consulted  
5. Foot link back to `obesity.html`

Combine, reorder, rename, or omit headings as the intervention requires. Do not force a rigid template against the object. Do not paste Thinking Artifact vocabulary. Source-check participation and safety bullets against the accepted package for that object. Prefer clinician questions over inventing counseling.

**Required headings when those sections exist:**

- Evidence: **What the evidence shows**  
- Combined population/expectation: **Who was studied, expectations, and limits**

**Shape lock on topic pages:** Preserve the six evidentiary voices. Medicines lead with comparative weight/harm, then the heart-outcome divider and SELECT population. Procedures keep four tensions in one first movement. Eating patterns force 12-month fade and the formula-diet branch to travel with six-month ranks. Exercise keeps three jobs. Behavioral programs force regain and lasting difference to travel together. Supplements do not imitate the others; the closed promotion gate is the finding.
