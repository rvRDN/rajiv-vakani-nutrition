# Peptide and Adjacent Therapies: Phase 1 Landscape Shortlist

**Status:** Phase 1 complete. MOTS-c placement resolved Jul 2026 (thin). KPV promoted Aug 2026 (thin). Jul 2026 PCAC votes recorded Aug 2026.  
**Version:** v0.2  
**Date:** July 15, 2026 (regulatory / KPV update Aug 11, 2026)  
**Continuity:** `docs/peptide-therapies-continuity.md`  
**Phase 1.5 brief:** `drafts/peptide-therapies-phase1.5-brief.md`  
**Prior draft:** `drafts/peptide-therapies-landscape-phase1-v0.1.md`

**Purpose:** Identify a workable shortlist for deeper evidence review. Not a comprehensive scan, not advocacy, and not a publication design.

**Stance:** Neutral. Understand the evidence well enough for informed conversations with patients, clinicians, and researchers.

**Mission filter:** Prefer therapies a knowledgeable patient could reasonably ask an RD about over “most scientifically interesting” or “most likely to succeed.”

**Stage notes:** Overview-level as of mid-2026. Verify labels, ClinicalTrials.gov, FDA notices, and peer-reviewed sources in Phase 2.

---

## Inclusion criteria

Candidates appear if they meet one or more of:

1. Advanced human clinical development  
2. FDA approved, or a credible near-term approval path  
3. Frequently discussed in clinical practice  
4. Significant ongoing research activity  
5. High patient interest  
6. Meaningful implications for nutrition, metabolism, body composition, or recovery  
7. Likelihood that a knowledgeable patient could reasonably ask an RD about it  

This is a shortlist, not an exhaustive inventory. Restraint is intentional.

---

## How to read the shortlist

Each dossier candidate is capped at five fields:

- **Mechanism**  
- **Primary indications** (approved, studied, or commonly claimed)  
- **Current development stage**  
- **Why it matters**  
- **Reason for inclusion**  

Grouped by scientific neighborhood, not predicted clinical value.

## Cluster A. Incretin and multi-agonist metabolic peptides

**Decision: Keep all. Do not touch.**

This cluster is the evolution of incretin therapy. Alone, it covers most of where obesity medicine is heading over the next 5–10 years.

### 1. Semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus)

- **Mechanism:** GLP-1 receptor agonist. Slows gastric emptying, increases satiety signaling, improves glucose-dependent insulin secretion.
- **Primary indications:** Type 2 diabetes; chronic weight management; expanded conversations around cardiovascular risk reduction, CKD progression, and heart failure with obesity where studied or labeled.
- **Current development stage:** FDA approved (injectable and oral diabetes formulations; injectable obesity formulations). Ongoing indication-expansion research.
- **Why it matters:** Anchors the clinical and public conversation. Sets the evidence bar for later compounds (efficacy, safety, nutrition implications, lean-mass loss, rebound, GI effects).
- **Reason for inclusion:** Approved; ubiquitous in practice; high patient interest; central to metabolism and nutrition counseling.

### 2. Tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound)

- **Mechanism:** Dual GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist.
- **Primary indications:** Type 2 diabetes; chronic weight management; obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity.
- **Current development stage:** FDA approved. Large outcome and expansion programs continuing.
- **Why it matters:** Dual-agonist comparator for next-generation agents. Body-composition and appetite effects force practical questions about protein, training, micronutrients, and maintenance.
- **Reason for inclusion:** Approved; advanced clinical use; high patient and clinician interest; body-composition relevance.

### 3. Retatrutide

- **Mechanism:** Triple agonist at GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon receptors. Glucagon agonism adds energy-expenditure and hepatic-fat mobilization hypotheses beyond dual agonists.
- **Primary indications:** Investigational obesity and type 2 diabetes; related metabolic comorbidities under study.
- **Current development stage:** Phase 3. Not FDA approved as of July 2026. Possible NDA trajectory later in 2026–2027 depending on datasets and review.
- **Why it matters:** Likely next major comparator if magnitude and safety hold. Raises questions about greater weight loss, liver fat, muscle preservation, and nutritional support needs.
- **Reason for inclusion:** Advanced human development; high legitimate scientific interest; metabolism and body-composition implications; high future patient-ask likelihood.

### 4. Cagrilintide / CagriSema (cagrilintide + semaglutide)

- **Mechanism:** Cagrilintide is a long-acting amylin analogue. CagriSema combines amylin and GLP-1 pathways.
- **Primary indications:** Investigational chronic weight management; diabetes programs also studied.
- **Current development stage:** Phase 3 complete for key obesity programs; NDA submitted; FDA decision anticipated late 2026 (company guidance, not a guarantee). Not approved as of July 2026.
- **Why it matters:** Tests whether adding amylin signaling improves outcomes beyond GLP-1 alone, and how combination tolerability and nutrition counseling differ.
- **Reason for inclusion:** Near-term approval path; advanced development; high clinical interest in combination incretin/amylin strategy.

### 5. Survodutide

- **Mechanism:** Dual GLP-1 and glucagon receptor agonist (no GIP).
- **Primary indications:** Investigational obesity and MASH (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis).
- **Current development stage:** Late Phase 3; SYNCHRONIZE-1 and SYNCHRONIZE-MASLD public; LIVERAGE histology ongoing; not approved (Jul 2026). Studio folio: `emerging-therapies/survodutide.html` **v0.1 frozen**.
- **Why it matters:** GLP-1/glucagon dual strategy with particular attention to liver fat. Different trade-offs than GIP-containing dual agonists.
- **Reason for inclusion:** Advanced development; significant research activity; metabolism and liver-nutrition implications.

## Cluster B. Adjacent comparator (non-peptide)

Included because it clarifies counseling questions, even though it is not a peptide.

### 6. Orforglipron — Adjacent comparator

- **Mechanism:** Oral small-molecule GLP-1 receptor agonist (non-peptide).
- **Primary indications:** Chronic weight management (confirm current FDA label, brand naming, dosing, and warnings in Phase 2); diabetes filings may follow separately.
- **Current development stage:** Reported FDA approved for weight management in 2026; verify against primary FDA/product sources.
- **Why it matters:** Important control. Helps answer whether benefits come from being a peptide, or from activating GLP-1. Also changes access and adherence dynamics versus injectables.
- **Reason for inclusion:** Adjacent comparator with high clinical and patient interest; pathway vs molecule distinction is central to future counseling conversations.

## Cluster C. Growth hormone axis peptides

### 7. Tesamorelin

**Decision: Keep. Anchor compound.**

- **Mechanism:** Growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) analogue. Stimulates endogenous GH release, raising IGF-1 and favoring visceral adipose reduction in studied populations.
- **Primary indications:** FDA approved for reduction of excess abdominal fat in HIV-associated lipodystrophy. Broader body-composition interest is an evidence question, not an approval fact.
- **Current development stage:** FDA approved for the labeled HIV lipodystrophy indication.
- **Why it matters:** Gives GH-related discussions something with an approved indication and real clinical data. That comparison will recur.
- **Reason for inclusion:** Approved anchor; body-composition relevance; legitimate comparator for GH-peptide conversations.

### 8. GH Secretagogue Class (Ipamorelin, CJC-1295, etc.)

**Decision: Keep. Name the compounds patients actually ask about.**

Patients do not ask for “growth hormone secretagogues.” They ask: “My friend is taking ipamorelin.”

- **Mechanism:** Stimulate GH release via ghrelin receptor (GHS-R1a; e.g., ipamorelin) and/or GHRH receptor pathways (e.g., CJC-1295 forms). Often discussed as stacks.
- **Primary indications:** No FDA-approved obesity, aging, or athletic indications for common wellness-marketed uses.
- **Current development stage:** Not FDA approved for those uses. Compounding and regulatory status contested. Often encountered via compounding or gray-market channels.
- **Why it matters:** Extreme volume in patient and clinic conversations about fat loss, muscle, sleep, and recovery. Educational core is evidence, regulation, product quality, and monitoring—not marketing claims.
- **Reason for inclusion:** High RD-ask likelihood by compound name; body-composition and recovery implications; regulatory and evidence-gap teaching value.

**Note:** Sermorelin can live inside this class review unless capacity justifies a separate dossier. Exogenous rhGH may appear as a comparator section later, not as a primary peptide dossier.

## Cluster D. Regenerative / injury and recovery peptides

### 9. BPC-157

**Decision: Keep. Non-negotiable for project purpose.**

Not because it is assumed to work. Because omitting it nearly misses one of the biggest reasons this project started.

- **Mechanism:** Synthetic gastric-related peptide fragment. Preclinical literature proposes angiogenesis, anti-inflammatory, and tissue-repair effects. Human mechanism claims remain incompletely validated.
- **Primary indications:** Commonly discussed for tendon, ligament, muscle, gut lining, and wound recovery. Claim/use patterns, not FDA-approved indications.
- **Current development stage:** Not FDA approved. Thin human evidence historically; early trial and compounding/PCAC attention in 2026. Jul 23, 2026: PCAC recommended 503A inclusion 8–6–1 after staff recommended against; rulemaking unfinished as of Aug 2026. Compounding eligibility is not drug approval.
- **Why it matters:** Among the most requested recovery peptides. The gap between preclinical enthusiasm, patient expectations, and human evidence is the educational topic.
- **Reason for inclusion:** High patient interest; recovery relevance; central to why the repository exists.

### 10. TB-500 / Thymosin β-4 related fragments

**Decision: Keep. Paired encounter pattern with BPC-157.**

- **Mechanism:** Related to thymosin beta-4 pathways (actin dynamics, cell migration, tissue remodeling). Product identity between research-marketed “TB-500” and pharmaceutical-grade thymosin β-4 needs careful review.
- **Primary indications:** Discussed for soft-tissue recovery, wound healing, and inflammation. Not FDA approved for popular uses.
- **Current development stage:** Not FDA approved for marketed recovery claims. Jul 23, 2026: PCAC recommended 503A inclusion 8–6–1 after staff recommended against; rulemaking unfinished as of Aug 2026.
- **Why it matters:** Patients often encounter it alongside BPC-157. Hard to discuss one without the other.
- **Reason for inclusion:** High patient interest; paired use pattern; recovery narrative; evidence and product-identity problems worth documenting.

## Cluster E. Thin investigations

### 11. MOTS-c: Placement resolved (thin investigation)

**Decision (Jul 15, 2026):** Graduate as a **thin** Emerging Therapies investigation. Not a watchlist one-liner. Not an equal-length dossier.

Evidence depth: substantial preclinical MDP / AMPK literature; observational endogenous human biology; CB4211 analogue Phase 1 (distinct object); thin published exogenous native efficacy package. Identity literacy (endogenous ≠ injectable ≠ analogue) earns wall space. Maturity matches evidence.

See: `drafts/emerging-therapies/mots-c-evidence-landscape-v0.1.md`, `emerging-therapies/mots-c.html`.

Jul 23, 2026 regulatory update: PCAC recommended 503A inclusion 7–5–2 after staff recommended against; rulemaking unfinished as of Aug 2026. Vote ≠ approval ≠ efficacy.

### 12. KPV: Thin investigation (promoted Aug 2026)

**Decision:** Graduate as a **thin** Emerging Therapies investigation. Not a watchlist one-liner. Not an equal-length dossier. Epitalon, Semax, and emideltide/DSIP stay deferred.

- **Mechanism:** C-terminal tripeptide of α-MSH (Lys-Pro-Val). Preclinical gut work frames PepT1 uptake and NF-κB quieting, largely independent of melanocortin-receptor pigmentation signaling.
- **Primary indications:** Marketed for gut inflammation, IBD, and skin. Claim/use patterns, not FDA-approved indications.
- **Current development stage:** Not FDA approved. No published patient trial of unmodified KPV identified. Jul 23, 2026: PCAC recommended 503A inclusion 8–6–1 after staff recommended against (staff: no human exposure data identified). Rulemaking unfinished as of Aug 2026.
- **Why it matters:** Reader search plus a live compounding vote. Identity traps (KdPT, α-MSH, “human cells”) are the teaching edge. Empty human file is the page, not a problem to hide.
- **Reason for inclusion:** Demonstrated consumer interest; nutrition-adjacent gut claims; regulatory hook; sparse careful explanatory coverage. PCAC membership alone would not have been enough.

See: `drafts/emerging-therapies/kpv-evidence-landscape-v0.1.md`, `emerging-therapies/kpv.html`.
