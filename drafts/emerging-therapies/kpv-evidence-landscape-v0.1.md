**Narrative the landscape supports:**

KPV is a three-amino-acid fragment with a scientifically coherent anti-inflammatory story in the lab. Clinics and forums sell it for gut inflammation, IBD, and skin. The published human file for *this* molecule is empty. Public conversation often borrows a different analogue’s ulcerative-colitis trial, or treats keratinocyte and airway-cell experiments as “human data.”

A July 2026 advisory-committee vote recommended compounding eligibility. That is a regulatory event. It is not a finding that KPV works in people.

## 1. Mechanism (landscape role)

α-MSH is a 13-amino-acid melanocortin peptide. KPV is only the C-terminal Lys-Pro-Val tail. It does not contain the His-Phe-Arg-Trp pharmacophore that drives melanocortin-receptor / pigmentation signaling in the parent hormone.

In the key gut paper (Dalmasso et al., *Gastroenterology* 2008), KPV entered intestinal epithelial and immune-cell models via the peptide transporter PepT1, which is upregulated in inflamed colon, and reduced NF-κB / MAPK inflammatory signaling. Oral KPV in drinking water reduced disease severity in DSS- and TNBS-induced mouse colitis. The paper framed the effect as PepT1-mediated, not melanocortin-receptor-mediated.

**Landscape meaning:** Explains why IBD and “anti-inflammatory without the tan” conversations notice it. Mechanism does not equal a labeled drug or a human package.

---

## 2. Human evidence split (critical)

### A. Unmodified KPV in people

No published interventional trial putting KPV into human participants was identified at freeze. ClinicalTrials.gov returned no registered KPV studies (API search Aug 11, 2026). FDA’s Jul 2026 KPV briefing (media 193346, pp. 21, 26, 29) stated the agency had not identified any clinical studies or human exposure data for KPV via any route of administration, and proposed not adding free base or acetate to the 503A Bulks List.

That emptiness *is* the clinical fact. Do not soften it.

### B. Human cells (not patients)

In vitro work in human cell lines (intestinal, immune, airway, keratinocyte) is real preclinical evidence. Citing it as “KPV has been tested in humans” is an identity error.

### C. KdPT / K(D)PT (distinct object)

KdPT (Lys-D-Pro-Thr) is a related but different tripeptide analogue, with a different proposed mechanism (IL-1 receptor neighborhood rather than the KPV PepT1 story). A European randomized trial in mild-to-moderate ulcerative colitis studied K(D)PT (EudraCT 2011-002462-20). That trial is adjacent family context. It is **not** a KPV trial and does not validate gray-market KPV vials.

### D. Parent hormone / tanning peptides

α-MSH and melanocortin agonists (including products people know as “melanotan”) are a different pharmacology. KPV’s marketing pitch is often “the anti-inflammatory fragment without pigmentation.” Do not transfer parent-hormone clinical claims to KPV, and do not treat KPV as a tanning peptide.

## 3. Claim environment

What people claim:

- Gut inflammation, “leaky gut,” ulcerative colitis, Crohn disease  
- Skin: eczema, psoriasis, wound healing  
- Systemic anti-inflammatory / immune “reset”  
- Cleaner than BPC-157; stacks with BPC-157 (sometimes as part of a named clinic stack)  
- “Human data exist” (often KdPT, or cell lines)

What the landscape supports:

- Coherent preclinical anti-inflammatory story, especially mouse colitis  
- Empty published patient package for unmodified KPV  
- Contested compounding / gray-market access  
- A 2026 compounding-eligibility vote that does not change efficacy

---

## 4. Regulatory / access / sport

- Not FDA-approved  
- Jul 23, 2026 PCAC: FDA staff recommended against 503A Bulks List inclusion; committee voted 8 yes, 6 no, 1 abstention to recommend inclusion (free base and acetate)  
- Votes are advisory. 503A compounding remains unlawful until a substance has a USP/NF monograph, is a component of an FDA-approved drug, or is added to the Bulks List by rulemaking  
- As of August 11, 2026: no proposed rule identified; legal status unchanged  
- Compounding eligibility ≠ drug approval ≠ evidence of benefit  
- WADA: not named as a specific entry on the 2026 Prohibited List in sources checked. Unapproved peptides can still be a problem under non-approved-substance rules. Tested athletes should ask their sport body, not treat silence as clearance

## 5. RD-ask / mission filter

Gut and inflammation questions are inside nutrition counseling in a way Epitalon and Semax are not. Patients will meet KPV next to BPC-157. The notebook earns its place by teaching **object literacy** (KPV ≠ KdPT ≠ α-MSH) and **vote literacy** (committee recommendation ≠ legal compounding ≠ it works). Day-to-day RD volume may stay modest. Keep the folio short.

Plant-based analogue: not applicable. KPV is a synthetic fragment of a human hormone, not an animal food product with a plant substitute.

---

## 6. What an RD / clinician needs

1. Name the molecule. KPV is not KdPT, not α-MSH, not BPC-157.  
2. Do not treat PepT1 / NF-κB slides as IBD outcome proof.  
3. If someone cites an ulcerative-colitis trial, ask which peptide.  
4. Established IBD and skin care still own those goals.  
5. The July 2026 vote is worth explaining once, then dropping. It does not upgrade the evidence.
