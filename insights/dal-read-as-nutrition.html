Dal, Read as Nutrition

Essay June 8, 2026 Draft

A draft in progress. The structure is in place; I'm still working on the prose. This page exists so the article has a home in the Library while I'm writing the piece.

Dal is the food South Asians get credited with most often and misread most often. To outsiders it's “Indian lentil soup,” shorthand for vegetarian protein and a checklist item on a healthy plate. Inside the cuisine it's a daily meal with a particular role, paired with particular other foods, eaten in particular ways. This piece treats dal not as tradition and not as a generic legume, but as nutrition, read closely.

What is in dal

A short field guide. The lentil families that get called dal: toor (arhar), moong, masoor, urad, chana. What each one contributes by weight: protein, fiber, micronutrients, and the specific carbohydrate mix that distinguishes them from grains. Soaking, splitting, and cooking. What the cooking does that matters and what it doesn't.

Protein, considered carefully

The protein in dal is a real macronutrient contribution, but it's not the same as a serving of paneer or chicken, and treating it that way is where the misreading starts. Amino acid profile. Digestibility. Why rice and dal together do most of the work the parts can't do separately.

Fiber and fermentation

The part of dal that gets discussed least and may matter most. The fermentable carbohydrate fraction. What it does in the gut. Why fermentation is part of the appeal of dal as a daily food, not just a side effect of eating it.

Glycemic response, in context

What a bowl of dal does to blood sugar on its own, what a meal of dal + rice does, and how the conversation gets distorted when one part of the meal is read without the other.

Where the misreadings come from

The summary frames that show up in nutrition writing about South Asian diets. “Vegetarian Indians are protein deficient.” “Dal-rice is too much starch.” What each of those gets almost right, and what they get wrong about how dal actually shows up in a meal.
