Carrageenan: What the Human Evidence Actually Shows

Research Review June 8, 2026 Draft

A draft in progress. The reading is mostly done; the writing is not. This page exists so the article has a home in the Library while the piece is being written.

Carrageenan shows up in plant milks, ice cream, infant formula, and a growing list of ultraprocessed foods. It also shows up on lists of ingredients to avoid, often near the top, often with confident language. The piece begins from that gap. What the evidence actually says about carrageenan in food, read carefully, and what any of it justifies concluding.

What carrageenan is

A polysaccharide extracted from red seaweed, used as a thickener and stabilizer. Several forms. The food-grade form is high molecular weight; the form used in some animal studies of inflammation is degraded carrageenan, also called poligeenan, which is not used in food. The distinction matters more than most summaries acknowledge.

What regulators have concluded

The FDA, EFSA, and the JECFA have all reviewed carrageenan as a food additive. Their conclusions, the dates of the most recent reviews, and the evidence each weighted. Where they agree, where they don’t.

The human evidence

What human studies exist. What endpoints they measured. The population sizes, durations, and limitations. Where the evidence is strong enough to support a claim, where it is weak, where it is absent.

The mechanism work
