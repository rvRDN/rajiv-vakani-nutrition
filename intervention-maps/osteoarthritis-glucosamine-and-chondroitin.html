Does glucosamine rebuild cartilage?

Bottom line

Independent patient-level data show glucosamine is no better than placebo for hip or knee osteoarthritis at three months or two years. No subgroup rescued that finding, including the type of glucosamine and whether the joint was a knee or a hip. That is not evidence of cartilage repair. Chondroitin is a different evidence story. One European hand guideline still allows it conditionally for hand symptoms. The main U.S. rheumatology guideline recommends against both for knee and hip. This isn't turmeric.

What this is

Glucosamine and chondroitin are widely sold as joint supplements, often in the same bottle. The marketing story is cartilage food. The evidence question is whether pain or function improves compared with placebo, and whether the joint itself changes.

They are not one finding. Glucosamine has an independent patient-level analysis for hip and knee. Chondroitin has a different evidence base, including a hand-specific guideline sentence. Curcumin is a different bottle again.

What the glucosamine evidence shows

Researchers pooled individual patient data from five independent placebo-controlled trials, 1,625 people in all. Glucosamine was not better than placebo at three months or at two years.

Pain level, body weight, sex, joint structure, signs of inflammation, knee versus hip, and type of glucosamine did not change that result.

Many industry-sponsored trials did not share their data for this analysis. That doesn't prove those unshared results would have been positive. It does mean the best independent shared data do not show a benefit.

Chondroitin is a different story

The main U.S. rheumatology guideline strongly recommends against glucosamine and against chondroitin for knee and hip osteoarthritis.

A European hand-osteoarthritis guideline still allows chondroitin conditionally for hand symptoms. That sentence is about chondroitin and the hand. It doesn't make glucosamine a knee cartilage drug, and it doesn't export a hand finding onto the hip or knee.

Shellfish, fermented, and combination pills

Many glucosamine products are made from shellfish. Fermented, non-shellfish versions exist. The independent analysis did not find a benefit for glucosamine over placebo, and the type of glucosamine did not change that. There isn't a separate fermented/non-shellfish glucosamine trial that reverses this finding.

A combination pill is not automatically a different evidence story. Combining two supplements doesn't turn a glucosamine null into cartilage repair.

What taking part usually involves

  • A daily capsule, often for months, bought over the counter.
  • Cost that adds up even when the independent hip and knee data show no benefit over placebo.
  • A check against what you hoped the bottle would do: pain, function, or “rebuilding the joint.”

What this does not do

Glucosamine hasn't been shown to rebuild cartilage. The independent patient-level analysis found no benefit for pain or function; it does not provide a separate reason to infer cartilage repair.

This also isn't a disease-modifying drug. And it isn't the turmeric question.

Ask your clinician

Useful questions

Start here: “If independent data show glucosamine is no better than placebo for hip or knee pain, is there a reason to keep taking it in my case?”

Also worth asking:

  • If the painful joint is a hand, are we talking about chondroitin, or about glucosamine?
  • What would we stop if nothing changes?
  • If the goal is cartilage repair, what actually has evidence for that?
