Rheumatoid arthritis: where each option sits in the evidence

Some rheumatoid arthritis treatments target the disease itself. Others help with symptoms, movement, or day-to-day function. This map shows where each option fits, what it can realistically do, and where the evidence becomes less certain.

Use this map to understand the options and prepare better questions for your rheumatology team. It isn't a guide to starting, stopping, or changing medication on your own.

See the different roles these options play. Not a menu. A landscape.

Treatments that control the underlying inflammatory disease and help prevent joint damage are called disease-modifying treatments. That's why they form the foundation of this map. Osteoarthritis and other kinds of arthritis are separate maps.

01

Treating the disease

Options that aim to control inflammatory disease and protect joints.

  • What is usually the first disease-modifying plan? Conventional DMARDs · often centered on methotrexate, with regular checks to see whether treatment is controlling the disease
  • What if the first plan isn't enough? Biologics and JAK inhibitors · options when conventional DMARDs aren't enough · JAK inhibitors require extra attention to certain health risks
02

Helping while treatment takes effect

Can help control symptoms while longer-term treatment takes effect, but usually isn't meant to become the long-term plan.

  • Are steroids a fix or a bridge? Glucocorticoids · short-term help beside disease-modifying treatment · plan to taper · not long-term disease control alone
03

Movement and daily function

Two different jobs on one page: training, and rehabilitation supports.

  • Does exercise help? Is PT or OT the same thing? Exercise and rehabilitation · exercise can build strength and capacity; OT, PT, braces, and other supports help with different problems in daily function
04

Food and supplements

These options don't all have the same evidence or do the same thing. None replaces treatment that controls the underlying disease.

  • Will changing how I eat treat RA? Diet and nutrition · Mediterranean-style eating has the clearest guideline support among formal patterns · not a replacement for treatment that controls the disease
  • Is fish oil worth considering for symptoms? Fish oil · a small benefit for pain or symptoms is possible · it does not replace treatment that controls RA
  • Which herbals have any evidence? What about turmeric? Herbal and botanical options · evidence and safety differ by product
