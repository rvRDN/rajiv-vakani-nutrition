Diet and nutrition

Bottom line

Diet and nutrition can sit alongside disease-modifying RA treatment. Mediterranean-style eating has the clearest guideline support among formal patterns. Some diets and nutrition interventions may modestly improve symptoms or disease activity, but the evidence is uncertain and generally low to very low in certainty. Diet and supplements do not replace treatment that controls RA itself.

What this option is

This page is about overall eating patterns and nutrition counseling for people who already have RA, not about preventing RA before it starts. It is not the same decision as taking fish oil pills.

For the narrow fish-oil question, see fish oil.

What the evidence shows

Among formal eating patterns, Mediterranean-style eating has the clearest guideline support for people with RA. It can be used alongside medical treatment, but that does not mean food has been shown to control RA the way disease-modifying medicines do.

Other named therapeutic diets and many nutritional interventions have been studied. An umbrella review finds possible modest signals for some of them, but certainty is generally low or very low. Those possible benefits are not strong enough to support using diet instead of indicated disease-modifying treatment.

Guidelines also do not support using dietary supplements as a strategy to control RA itself.

What taking part usually involves

  • Shifting toward a Mediterranean-style eating pattern, adapted to your culture, preferences, budget, and nutritional needs.
  • Time, cooking skills, cost, and household buy-in. Dropout and weight change are real practical issues in diet trials.
  • Shared expectations: symptom comfort and general health may move; disease modification is not the claim.

Common confusions

“Any anti-inflammatory diet inherits the Mediterranean support.” It does not. Formal patterns are judged separately.

“If I eat perfectly, I can skip DMARDs.” Diet does not earn that replacement.

“Supplements are the nutrition plan.” Supplements do not have the same guideline support for controlling RA itself.

Ask your clinician

Useful questions

Start here: “Would a Mediterranean-style eating pattern make sense for me, and what should I realistically expect from it?”

Also worth asking:

  • Would a restrictive diet add anything worth the burden?
  • Am I at risk of missing any nutrients with the way I currently eat?
  • Would working with a dietitian be useful?
  • How should I think about supplements marketed for RA?
